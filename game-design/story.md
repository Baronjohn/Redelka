# Story

The narrative arc, protagonists, antagonist, and chapter shape for **Redelka**. Setting rules (era, geography, folk-horror flavor) live in [world.md](world.md); narrative delivery methods (cutscenes, dialogue, banter, environmental storytelling) live in [presentation.md](presentation.md). This doc focuses on **what the story is**, not how it is rendered.

## Premise

A remote village is going quiet. People vanish from their beds, their fields, their usual haunts — one or two at a time, never enough to panic, always enough to notice. What remains is fear and routine, both thinning.

Four strangers with nothing in common — except, eventually, the missing — start pulling at the same thread. By the end they understand the absences are not crimes but **fuel**. A forgotten rite is being completed in the dark, fed by kidnapped villagers, and an Arch Demon is gathering enough souls and sacrifices to be reborn into the world. The land has already begun to sicken from the smaller horrors the rite is calling up.

The story follows their investigation from the first empty sleeping spot to the final ritual chamber.

## Tone

**Decided:**

- **Classic survival horror** register: isolation, scarcity, unglamorous violence.
- References: original *Koudelka*, early *Resident Evil*, *Silent Hill 2* — dread of place and self before dread of spectacle.
- Folk-horror trappings (old rites, pagan belief treated as real) carry the weight; monsters are not the centerpiece, the **community that lets them happen** is.
- Restraint in writing: short sentences, plain speech. Horror comes from what's not said.

## Protagonists

**Decided:** Four co-protagonists with distinct reasons to care. **Mira is the POV protagonist** — the story opens with her alone and stays primarily in her voice (sparse, internal; dialogue carries most of the plot).

### Mira (POV, starts alone)

- Late-teen street orphan, raised on the margins of the village.
- Her stake: she is searching for a **missing orphan** she helped out — a younger child who shared her sleeping spots and scraps. Mira feels directly responsible; she let the kid down.
- Personality: defiant, hot-tempered, suspicious, loyal once committed. Speaks in dry, plain sentences.
- Role in combat: agile knife-fighter, back-line harasser.

### Owen

- Hunter-gatherer in his thirties, away from the village when the trouble began.
- His stake: he has found **strange ritual sites** out in the woods and the high places — places no one in the village admits to. He investigates because he cannot leave a pattern alone.
- Personality: gentle, steady, patient, observant. Speaks plainly, thinks before talking.
- Role in combat: ranged support, covers allies, picks targets with a hunter's patience.

### Bran

- Former prison guard in his forties.
- His stake: in the course of his work he has heard **frightening reports from convicted criminals** — confessions, ravings, or warnings he couldn't dismiss. He has carried those reports with him; the disappearances make them fit.
- Personality: stoic, reserved, duty-driven, protective. Few words, blunt structure.
- Role in combat: frontline anchor, holds the line.

### Elara

- Self-taught mage in her mid-twenties, from the village's richer family.
- Her stake: none, at first. She **stumbles into Mira by chance** in chapter 1 and follows her out of curiosity — and because she has been reading texts she shouldn't have, and she wants to see whether they are true.
- Personality: secretive, disciplined, analytical, withholding. Precise diction; reassuring only when she decides you are ready.
- Role in combat: back-line spellcaster.

### How they meet

**Decided:**

- Mira is the only character present at the very start.
- The party forms **across the course of the story**, not in a single meeting beat.
- Owen and Elara **loosely know each other** before the story begins; no one has met Bran before.
- Early encounters are guarded; trust is earned in blood, not words.

## The antagonist

**Decided (placeholder names — see Open questions):**

- **The Arch Demon** (working name) is attempting to be reborn into the world. It is not present yet; it is being **prepared** by mortal hands.
- **The cult** (working name "the Bound" or similar) is a small, hidden group of villagers and outsiders who perform the **Rite of Return** (working name). They kidnap villagers to feed the rite as fuel.
- **Lesser horrors** are summoned as a byproduct of the rite's slow progress. They infest the surrounding woods, fields, and buildings. They are the enemies the party fights most often: humanoid, predatory, sad in the way a sick animal is sad.
- **The land itself** is changing. Crops fail. Animals go quiet. Water tastes wrong. The slow sickening is felt before it is seen.

### Layered antagonism

- The cult is **human**; destroying it does not end the rite, because the rite's momentum has already crossed a threshold.
- The rite is **ancient and impersonal**; destroying the cult does not undo the lesser horrors already summoned, nor the kidnapped villagers already lost.
- The Arch Demon is the **final, inhuman threat**; only a complete stop to the rite prevents the rebirth, at the cost of whatever that stopping demands.
- The party must therefore fight on **two fronts at once**: the cult's operatives in the present, and the rite's momentum in the long run.

### Tonal guidance for the antagonist

- Show the cult as **people with families**, not as obvious villains. Most of them believe they are saving the village by feeding it. A few know better.
- The Arch Demon should never fully appear until the climax. Its presence is felt through the land, the lesser horrors, and the rite itself.
- No random evil. Every monster was a person, an animal, or a place once. The horror is what has been done to them.

## Magic and the rite

**Decided:**

- Spells in the game are **fragments of the forbidden rite** the cult is trying to complete. Elara found old texts — forbidden, partially destroyed — and decodes them through the story.
- Each spell the party learns is also **a piece of what the cult wants**: a fragment of a forbidden working. Learning the spell is useful; it is also unsettling, because Elara is teaching the party the same language the rite is written in.
- This ties directly to [progression.md — Spells](progression.md): spell unlocks happen through story progress, encounter victories, and Elara's study. Tier 0 = locked; tiers 1–3 are earned by use.
- **Skill** (character-specific) mechanics are **TBD** in [progression.md](progression.md) but should also pull from this lore where appropriate.

## Chapter structure

**Decided:**

- 8–10 chapters total (matches [overview.md — Scope](overview.md)).
- **Rising investigation with side branches**: chapters are mostly linear in story beats, but optional areas, secrets, and side rooms deepen lore without changing main outcomes.
- **Escalation curve**: *quiet dread → open violence → apocalyptic crescendo*.
  - Early chapters: local, personal, missing-people scale. Enemies are lone or paired.
  - Middle chapters: the cult's operation is exposed. Enemies come in prepared ambushes.
  - Late chapters: the rite is in motion. Enemies are numerous and the land is visibly hostile.
  - Final chapter: the ritual site. Boss fights, no retreat, the rite is either stopped or completed.

### Chapter 1 — The Missing

**Decided (this chapter is the first to be built):**

A short introduction to the world and the four characters. The game opens with Mira, alone, at her usual sleeping spot in the village — an abandoned doorway, a corner of a roofed market, somewhere she and the other orphan knew.

- **Mira is alone.** The player learns her through small environmental storytelling (her few possessions, her routine, the streets she knows).
- Short scenes introduce **Owen, Bran, and Elara separately**, before the party forms. Each is shown briefly, in their own element: Owen returning from a hunt, Bran on duty at the village prison, Elara alone in her family's library reading forbidden texts.
- **The inciting incident:** Mira searches for the missing orphan and finds her usual sleeping spot empty. Signs of a quiet struggle. A neighbor saw "a tall man" leading her toward the woods the night before.
- Mira follows the trail to the edge of the woods. Something is wrong there — the trees are quiet in the wrong way, the air is wrong.
- The chapter ends on a **hook, not a boss fight**. Mira returns to the village and the search begins.

## Narrative delivery

**Decided:** All methods listed in [presentation.md — Narrative delivery](presentation.md) are in scope:

- Real-time staged cutscenes on fixed cameras.
- Dialogue with **flavor choices** (mostly linear, with tone variation).
- Environmental storytelling through notes, documents, item descriptions, and the land itself.
- Party banter at rest points.

### Mira's voice

**Decided:** Mira narrates **sparingly, internally**, in plain short sentences. She is not silent — but she is not a poet either. Her voice is a working-class orphan's voice, often suspicious, occasionally tender, never ornamented. Other characters are introduced through **how she reads them**, not through third-person description.

## Ending

**TBD.** The doc commits to the following shape without committing to a specific resolution:

- The final chapter is set at the ritual site. No retreat.
- The rite can be **stopped**, but stopping it has a cost that the design will name (a sacrifice, a binding, a permanent wound, or a refusal of return).
- The Arch Demon's rebirth is the explicit threat; resolution depends on whether the rite's momentum was reversed in time.
- One canonical ending is the working assumption. Multiple endings from late-game choices are **TBD**.
